Methodology note: The current-law baseline uses the existing SSB weekly wage bands and the published dollar contribution amounts for each band. Annual payroll earnings are converted to weekly earnings first. For the proposed scenario, insurable earnings are then calculated from those weekly earnings subject to the selected floor and ceiling, then multiplied by the selected rates. The decomposition columns separate (1) replacing the wage-band midpoint with actual earnings while retaining the current $55 minimum IE, (2) raising that minimum to the selected proposed floor, and (3) changing the employee/employer split. Split effects use each worker’s current-band effective employee/employer shares as the counterfactual, so the components add exactly to the modeled net change. About this model

The BCCI SSB Contribution Impact Model is an interactive scenario-analysis tool for examining how alternative Social Security contribution structures affect a modeled payroll.

What it does

The model converts annual employee earnings to weekly earnings, applies the current weekly wage-band contribution schedule as the baseline, and compares that baseline with percentage-based scenarios using a selected contribution floor, ceiling, total contribution rate, and employee share. Employer share is calculated as the total rate less the employee share.

How to interpret results

The Employee Impact view separates changes in the total contribution pool from changes in who bears that cost. The Scenario Comparison view applies multiple policy assumptions to the same payroll so their aggregate effects can be compared on a consistent basis.

Data and limitations

Input earnings should be annual gross earnings. The model does not attempt to reproduce every special statutory contribution rule or employee circumstance. It is an analytical aid, not a payroll engine or legal interpretation. Users should verify assumptions and outputs before using them in formal analysis.

Privacy

Your payroll data remain in your browser. The model reads the Excel or CSV file you select and performs calculations locally in your browser. The tool does not upload the payroll file to BCCI or send the employee-level data to a server. Excel files are read in-browser using the SheetJS library.

If you export results, the resulting CSV is generated locally by your browser and downloaded to your device. Closing or refreshing the page clears the payroll loaded into the model.

As with any sensitive payroll information, users should follow their organization’s own data-handling and device-security procedures.
